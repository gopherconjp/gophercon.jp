# CFP Guidelines

GopherCon Japan is a bilingual (English & Japanese) Go conference held in Japan.  
We aim to create a space where people who use Go, people who are curious about Go, and people involved in the Go community can come together across languages to connect and learn from each other.

For Gophers in Japan, it's a chance to meet Gophers from overseas and connect with the global Go community.  
For Gophers outside Japan, it's a great opportunity to visit Japan and get to know the Go community in Japan.

Through this Call for Proposals, we're looking for speakers who will share their knowledge, their practice, their explorations, and their challenges with Go.  
There are no requirements regarding Go experience, speaking experience, native language, affiliation, or background. We welcome proposals from everyone, everywhere.

If this is your first time speaking at a conference, or if you'd like to try speaking in a non-native language, we'd especially love to hear from you.

## Types of Proposals

We're calling for three types of proposals for GopherCon Japan, listed below.

### Long Session

30-minute session.  
Long sessions are expected to share substantial material: technical insights into Go, real-world case studies, design and operation experience, deep dives into the internals of Go itself or the standard library, and more.

### Challenge Session

20-minute session.  
Challenge Sessions are for talks that involve stepping outside your comfort zone in some way. We especially welcome proposals from:

- Speakers taking on their first conference talk
- Speakers who want to try presenting in a non-native language

GopherCon Japan welcomes speakers who want to take on the language barrier challenge. In particular, we strongly encourage those who have rich experience speaking in Japanese to take on the challenge of presenting in English.

### Lightning Talk

A short talk, 5-7 minutes.  
We're looking for lightning talks that present something new to the audience: a small discovery, something you tried out, a lesson learned from a failure, an OSS project or tool you want to introduce, or community activities you'd like to share.

## About _Target_

At GopherCon Japan, we have defined five _targets_ to clarify the intended audience and the focus of your talk.  
This concept was introduced to help applicants clarify who their talk is for and what perspective on Go they are presenting.  
It also helps the evaluation team create a balanced and diverse schedule during the selection process.

Targets are categorized along two axes: **Difficulty** and **Domain**.

### Difficulty

Session difficulty is categorized into three levels: **Beginner**, **Intermediate**, and **Advanced**. This indicates the level of Go experience or knowledge expected of the audience.

### Domain

Session content is categorized into two main focus areas: **Use Case** and **Internal**.  
This indicates whether the talk primarily focuses on practical insights gained from real-world Go usage, or on technical curiosity regarding the inner workings of Go and its ecosystem.

_Note: Since most Advanced sessions are expected to cover both domains, they are not categorized by domain._

---

Below are the details for each target.  
When submitting your proposal, please select the target that best fits your talk.

### Use Case Beginner

Sessions focusing on practical Go use cases, learning paths, and development tips that are enjoyable and accessible for attendees with 0 to 2 years of Go experience.  
Expected topics include practical knowledge for Go beginners, insights gained during the learning process, introductions to useful tools and libraries, simple AI integrations, and entry points into OSS and community activities.  
For the Use Case Beginner target, we place particular value on personal narratives: why you are giving this talk, and the experiences or passion that inspired it.

### Use Case Intermediate

Sessions focusing on practical Go use cases and advanced development/operations insights, tailored for attendees with a solid background in Go development.  
Expected topics include using Go in large-scale systems, design and operational decision-making, performance tuning, observability, tooling, codebase improvements, and strategies for team development.  
For the Use Case Intermediate target, we welcome specialized knowledge — insights that are not well-known but can be applied in other settings, plus actionable takeaways attendees can bring to their own projects.

### Internal Beginner

Sessions focusing on Go internals — deep-dive sessions on how Go works, accessible and engaging for attendees with 0 to 2 years of Go experience or those who may not have a computer science background.  
We welcome foundational talks that assume a level of knowledge similar to the Tour of Go and carefully explain the core concepts from the ground up.  
Rather than focusing on fine implementation details, these talks should explain: "Why is it designed this way?" "What are the benefits?" "What does it do for us?" and "How should we conceptualize this?"  
For the Internal Beginner target, we welcome sessions that serve as an accessible gateway for beginners, while also offering fresh rediscoveries for experienced developers.

### Internal Intermediate

Sessions focusing on Go internals — deep-dive sessions on how Go works, tailored for attendees with a solid background in Go development and a foundational knowledge of computer science.  
Expected topics include the inner workings of features Gophers use daily, such as the Go runtime, standard library, testing frameworks, concurrency, memory management, and the toolchain.  
For the Internal Intermediate target, we welcome talks that articulate "How is it implemented?" "What is the background behind its design?" and "How can this understanding be applied in practice?"

### Advanced

Sessions focusing on advanced concepts, tailored for attendees with a high level of expertise in both Go and computer science.  
Expected topics include the most complex areas of Go: deep internal architectures of Go or software built with it, novel or unprecedented use cases, advanced tool development, `ast`, `unsafe`, ABIs, compilers, runtimes, OS-level interactions, testing infrastructure, and metaprogramming.  
For the Advanced target, we do not strictly separate Use Case from Internal sessions. At this level, a deep understanding of Go's internals is often closely connected to its practical application.

## Evaluation Criteria

Proposals will be reviewed primarily based on the following criteria:

### Relevance

Whether the content is related to Go, its ecosystem, development and operations using Go, or the Go community.

### Clarity

Whether the session content, intended audience, and key takeaways are clearly defined.

### Correctness

Whether the proposal shares accurate knowledge about Go, grounded in the submitter's own experience or research.

### Achievability

Whether the scope of the presentation is achievable within the selected time slot.

### Impact

Whether the session provides attendees with new ideas, technologies, tools, perspectives, or decision-making frameworks.

### Target Alignment

Whether the expected audience and depth of the content closely align with the selected target.

## Submission Guidelines & Requirements

- Up to two submissions are permitted per person, but only a single one will be accepted.
- All sessions must be delivered by a single speaker. Co-presentations or group presentations are not permitted.
- Proposals must be submitted in either Japanese or English.
- The presentation language must be either Japanese or English.
- Please indicate your preferred speaking language in the submission.
- If possible, please indicate if you are open to presenting in the other language as well.
- Your proposal content must be directly relevant to Go.
- Presentations primarily aimed at criticizing other languages, communities, specific individuals, or organizations are prohibited.
- Sales or marketing pitches, as well as presentations overly focused on product promotion, are strongly discouraged.
- The event will be photographed and recorded on video for promotional purposes. By submitting a proposal, you agree to being photographed and recorded, as well as to the use of these materials.
- All submitters are required to adhere to the [Go Community Code of Conduct](https://go.dev/conduct).

## A Final Word

GopherCon Japan aims to be an open platform connecting the Go community in Japan with the global Go community.

Whether you are a long-time Go veteran or someone who just started learning,  
whether you prefer to speak in Japanese or English,  
and whether you are based in Japan wanting to share your voice with the world, or joining from abroad to speak to Gophers in Japan — we welcome your proposal.

We invite you to share your experiences, insights, questions, explorations, and challenges at GopherCon Japan.  
We are looking forward to receiving your proposals!
